## Texas Driver License Process for McKinney Residents

The exact process depends on the applicant’s age, education method, permit status, and previous licensing history. Requirements can change; confirm current rules with the Texas Department of Public Safety and TDLR.

01

### Choose the Correct Education Path

Teen applicants may use an instructor-led program, an eligible online program with in-car instruction, or an approved Parent-Taught Driver Education course.

First-time applicants ages 18 through 24 generally need a six-hour adult driver education course. Adults age 25 or older generally do not.

02

### Obtain the Appropriate Learner License

An eligible teen can generally apply for a learner license beginning at age 15 after meeting the applicable education, identity, residency, school-enrollment, and testing requirements.

Adult beginners need the appropriate Texas restricted license before driving during an in-car lesson on public roads.

03

### Complete Behind-the-Wheel Training

Texas teen driver education includes seven hours of student driving and seven hours of in-car observation. Teen drivers also complete 30 additional supervised hours, including at least 10 hours at night.

Adult students complete the professional and lawful practice needed for their individual skill level.

Adult Driving Lessons

04

### Keep the Required Records

Teen and parent-taught students should retain the applicable DE-964, driving log, transfer records, and school documents.

Applicants ages 18 through 24 should retain the applicable ADE-1317 or another accepted driver education certificate.

05

### Complete Impact Texas Drivers

Teen applicants generally complete Impact Texas Teen Drivers. Adult applicants complete the Impact Texas program appropriate to their age and education path.

The certificate must be valid on the driving-test date.

06

### Pass the Driving Skills Test

Eligible students may test through DPS or a DPS-authorized Third-Party Skills Testing provider.

Passing the driving test satisfies the skills-test requirement. Texas DPS remains responsible for completing the application and issuing the driver license.
